Your Website Isn't a Storefront. It's a Sales Floor

Per “Pierre” Jørgensen

Let's say people walk into your store, poke around a bit, then leave without buying.

What do you do? Redesign the façade? Change your logo and get a new sign out front? Advertise to get more foot traffic?

Of course not. And yet, the online equivalent is to give your site a facelift and double down on SEO, and that is where too many businesses burn cash.

If someone says your home page needs a redesign, or the site needs a facelift, or the brand needs a refresh … call time out. Get down to basics: What problem are you trying to solve?

Can they trust it?

Imagine the parent shopping for healthy snacks for their kids: “These locally made granola bars look really good, but my kid has severe peanut allergies. I can see these are peanut free, but this is clearly a small business and the product page doesn't say if they were made right next to the peanut-butter ones.”

Do they understand what they're choosing?

Imagine shopping for your first e-bike: “I don't know what the difference between 'hub-drive' or 'mid-drive' is or what 'Class 1', 'Class 2', or 'Class 3' are. Help me!”

Can they find it?

Imagine the expert buyer coming in to get a part: “I can find the doohickeys, but I need one with inverted plinth valves that can inter-gasket wide flanges, and I need it to be compatible with an ACME hydro-torque rotorvator model 6-UH78. How do I find that?”

Are you getting in their way?

Imagine the buyer with a product already in cart, ready to hand you money: “I have to create an account to check out? Nope, not another user name and password. I'm out.”

Branding is valuable, but it alone will not fix any of those issues. You can, because you're too smart to fall in the “website facelift” trap, but you have to look past the look and feel to what's really going on with your site.

  • Pull the drop-off numbers between cart and checkout confirmation.
  • Watch ten people with a realistic purchase in mind navigating and shopping your site.
  • Talk to potential customers, not people who already bought from you or anyone who works there, and ask what they would expect to find.
  • Dig into the numbers: How many are dropping off between your home page and product pages? How many are browsing products but leaving without adding to cart? How many add to cart but abandon it? This will give you a better idea where the problem is so you can focus your efforts on fixing the right thing.

And yes, this work isn't as glamorous as branding, and it may cost more than a logo, but it's the thing that changes whether people buy.
